# When you have the initiative, keep making threats: no slow moves

## TL;DR

If you're the one making threats your opponent must answer, keep it going with forcing, active moves. A lead in development is temporary and fades if you play slowly.

## Why it matters

The thinking-process video: 'When you have initiative in chess, you need to keep playing with speed. You cannot play slow moves.' After winning a pawn, the author immediately played Bb5 to put a second attacker on a knight, and the attack kept rolling. Wikipedia defines the initiative as being able to make threats that can't be ignored, such as checks, so the opponent spends turns answering them instead of developing. It adds that temporary advantages, like a lead in development or active pieces, often need action right away before they disappear, unlike lasting ones such as a better pawn structure.

## How to apply

When your opponent is busy answering your threats, pick a next move that creates another threat: a check, an attack on a piece, or another attacker on a target. Ask whether your advantage is temporary (development, activity) or lasting (pawn structure, the bishop pair). If it's temporary, act now.

## Check yourself

You're ahead in development and have just forced your opponent's queen to retreat. You're choosing between a quiet pawn move on the edge of the board and a move that attacks one of their pieces. What does this lesson say, and why does timing matter?
