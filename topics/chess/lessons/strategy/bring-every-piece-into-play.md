# Bring every piece into the game before pushing ahead

## TL;DR

Don't leave pieces 'on the bench'. When you're unsure what to do, find a piece that isn't playing and give it an active job.

## Why it matters

In the thinking-process video, the cleanest winning line came from the principle 'play with all the pieces in chess, don't leave any piece on the bench'. Instead of a messier pawn move, White brought a rook to e1. Later, before pushing a pawn it could have pushed at once, White first activated its rook on a1 ('I don't want to play c4 and keep this rook on a1 looking at a pawn'), and the rook created immediate threats.

## How to apply

Before a pawn push, or a plan that uses only one piece, look at each of your pieces and ask whether it's doing anything. Move the least active one to a square where it attacks something or supports your plan (for example, a rook onto an open file), then carry on.

## Check yourself

You're a pawn up in a quiet position. You could push a pawn right now, but your rook is still in the corner behind its own pawns. What does this lesson suggest you do first?
