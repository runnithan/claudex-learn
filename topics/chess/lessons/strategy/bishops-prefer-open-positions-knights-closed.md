# Prefer bishops in open positions and knights in closed ones

## TL;DR

Bishops and knights are worth about the same, but bishops are stronger when lines are open and knights are stronger when pawns block the board. When deciding a trade, keep the piece that suits the position.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia: when bishops are blocked in by pawns in closed positions, knights are usually better because they can hop over pawn chains. In open positions where bishops have scope, knights are often worse, and a bishop pair can be worth seven points or more. The exception is a knight on an outpost.

## How to apply

Before trading a bishop for a knight or the other way round, look at the pawns. Many locked pawns favor the knight; open lines favor the bishop. Value the bishop pair highly in open positions, especially when the opponent has lost one or both of their bishops.

## Check yourself

The center is locked by interlocking pawn chains, and you can trade either your bishop or your knight for an enemy piece. Which piece does this lesson suggest keeping, and why?

## Caveat

The article isn't consistent about endgames. One passage says bishops are slightly better in open positions 'such as toward the end of the game when many of the pieces have been captured', while another says that in endgames bishops are usually considered equal to knights. Both passages agree on the open-versus-closed rule, so judge by how open the position is rather than by the phase of the game.
