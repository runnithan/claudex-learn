# Count material with the 1-3-3-5-9 piece values before trading

## TL;DR

Pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. Add up what you give and what you get before any capture or trade, and aim to take your opponent's pieces while keeping your own.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia says the most basic way to evaluate a position is to count the value of each side's pieces, and that a fundamental rule of both strategy and tactics is to capture the opponent's pieces while keeping your own. More pieces, or more powerful ones, generally means better chances of winning.

## How to apply

Some sums from the article: winning a rook for a knight or bishop ('winning the exchange') is worth about two pawns. Two minor pieces are stronger than one rook, and two rooks are slightly stronger than a queen. Three pawns are likely to be more useful than a knight in the endgame, but in the middlegame the knight is often stronger. Treat the values as a starting point: they shift with where the pieces stand (advanced pawns are worth more), how well they work together (the bishop pair), and the type of position.

## Check yourself

You can capture your opponent's rook with your bishop, and they'll recapture your bishop. Using this lesson's values, who comes out ahead, and by roughly how much?
