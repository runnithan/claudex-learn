# Keep your opponent's pieces as passive as you can

## TL;DR

Part of every plan is restricting the opponent. Don't let their pieces become active, and use checks and pawn advances to push them back.

## Why it matters

The thinking-process video: 'We always want to make our opponent's pieces as inactive as possible... We want to restrict his forces as much as possible.' Up material in an endgame, White used rook checks to drive the black king to the back rank and pushed passed pawns that tied Black's pieces down. Only once Black's pieces were passive did White start pushing for the win.

## How to apply

Each move, ask which enemy piece is about to become active (for example, a king heading for the center or a rook reaching an open file). Look for a move that stops it: a check that drives it back, a pawn that takes away its square, or a piece that controls its route. Then press on with your own plan.
