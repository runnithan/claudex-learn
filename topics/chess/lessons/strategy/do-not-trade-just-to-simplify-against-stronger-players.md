# Against a stronger player, don't swap pieces just to 'simplify'

## TL;DR

Swapping pieces just to simplify against a stronger opponent often helps them, because stronger players are often relatively stronger in the endgame, while mistakes are more common in a complicated middlegame.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia notes that many beginners facing stronger players keep exchanging pieces 'to simplify matters'. But stronger players are often relatively stronger in the endgame, while errors are more common in the more complicated middlegame.

## How to apply

Before a trade, ask what it does for you, not just whether it makes the position simpler. Trades are worth it when they serve a purpose: the article says they usually favor the side that's ahead in material, and as a rule of thumb they make things easier for a defender who has little room. Otherwise, keep the pieces on against a stronger player.

## Check yourself

You're playing a friend who's clearly stronger. Material is level and your opponent offers a trade of rooks. The trade doesn't win anything or relieve any pressure, but the position would feel simpler. What does this lesson suggest?
