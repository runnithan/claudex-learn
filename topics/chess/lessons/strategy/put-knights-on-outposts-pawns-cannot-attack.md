# Plant a knight on an outpost where enemy pawns can't attack it

## TL;DR

Knights are easily chased away by pawns, so look for a square in the enemy position that no enemy pawn can attack, ideally supported by one of your pawns, and put a knight there.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia: a knight on such a square on the fifth rank is a strong asset, and the ideal spot is the opponent's third rank, supported by one or two pawns. A central knight that can't be taken threatens several forks and can be worth far more than a bishop. The article's section on space adds that a protected piece behind enemy lines can become such a problem that the opponent has to give up a more valuable piece for it.

## How to apply

Look for 'holes' in the opponent's pawn structure: squares their pawns can no longer cover. Support the square with a pawn, then route a knight there. In your own camp, avoid pawn moves that create such holes.

## Check yourself

There's a square on your fifth rank that no enemy pawn can attack and that one of your pawns supports. Your knight is two moves away from it. What does this lesson say about that square?
