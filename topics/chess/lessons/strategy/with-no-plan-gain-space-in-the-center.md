# When you have no clear plan, make a move that gains space in the center

## TL;DR

If your pieces are developed and you see no tactic and no long-term plan, look for a move that increases your influence, especially in the center. Other things being equal, more space is an advantage.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia: the side with more space has more options, both tactical and strategic, and the other side is cramped. The easiest way to gain space is to push your pawns forward. The article gives Larry Evans's way to measure space: count the squares each side attacks or occupies on the opponent's half of the board. In its Nimzo-Indian example White has eight to Black's four, and Black is cramped.

## How to apply

1. Check that there's no tactic and no clear plan. 2. Look for a pawn push or piece move that increases the number of squares you control on the opponent's half, especially central ones. 3. Don't overstretch. Advanced pawns your pieces can't support become long-term weaknesses, and weak squares left behind them can host a protected enemy piece. (Some openings deliberately accept less space for a while to counterattack later.)
