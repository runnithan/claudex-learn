# Put your rooks on open and half-open files

## TL;DR

Rooks need open lines. Move them to files with none of your own pawns on them, and don't leave them stuck in the corner behind pawns.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia: rooks have more room to move on half-open files (files with none of your own pawns), and a rook is worth more when doubled with another rook or the queen, so doubled rooks are worth more than two unconnected rooks. In the opening video, once the rooks are connected, the presenter says Black would probably want both rooks on semi-open files to put pressure down them. In the thinking-process video, White saw that Black's plan was to bring a rook to the only open file, and before pushing c4 it first activated its own rook from a1 to b1 rather than leave it 'looking at a pawn'.

## How to apply

After connecting your rooks, look for files without your own pawns and put your rooks there, doubling them if you can. Also notice which open files your opponent wants, and think about contesting or controlling them.

## Check yourself

Your rooks are connected. One file has none of your pawns on it; another is blocked by your own pawn on the second rank. Where does this lesson say your rooks belong?
