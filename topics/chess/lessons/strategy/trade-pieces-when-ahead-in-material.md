# Trade pieces when you're ahead in material

## TL;DR

When you're up material, swap pieces off to simplify. Fewer pieces give the opponent fewer chances to fight back, and one extra pawn can decide an endgame.

## Why it matters

The thinking-process video: 'When you're up material in chess, you want to trade down as much as possible... it makes converting the winning position easier.' Two pawns up, White took on c6 to force trades, and later grabbed more pawns because each capture simplified the position further. Wikipedia: trading brings the endgame closer and leaves the opponent less chance to recover. Small material advantages can usually only be turned into a win in an endgame, where even a single extra pawn may be decisive.

## How to apply

When you're ahead, prefer captures and trades that keep your extra material. The exception, from Wikipedia: not every trade helps. If a trade would leave only bishops that run on opposite colors, the ending is often a draw even with one or two extra pawns, so avoid that particular simplification.

## Check yourself

You're up a pawn. You can trade queens or keep them on. Separately, one possible trade would leave you with a light-squared bishop against their dark-squared bishop. What does this lesson say about each?
