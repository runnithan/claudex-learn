# Avoid creating pawn weaknesses unless you get something for them

## TL;DR

Pawn moves can't be taken back, so weaknesses like isolated, doubled or backward pawns and holes usually last all game. Accept one only for real compensation, such as attacking chances.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia: pawns are the least mobile pieces, so the pawn structure changes little, and weaknesses in it are usually permanent once created. The article lists avoiding isolated, doubled and backward pawns and pawn islands among the basic aims of the opening. It also warns that pushing pawns too far for space can leave weak squares and targets behind.

## How to apply

Before a pawn move, or a recapture that changes your pawns, ask whether it creates a lasting weakness. If it does, make sure you get something valuable in return, such as a chance to attack. If you don't, choose another move.

## Check yourself

You can recapture with a pawn in a way that creates one of the pawn weaknesses the article lists, or with a piece that keeps your pawns healthy. Neither gives you an attack. Which does this lesson point you toward?
