## Openings

- [Occupy the center with pawns in the opening](openings/occupy-the-center-with-pawns.md) (beginner): Early in the game, put pawns on the central squares when your opponent lets you.
- [Develop your knights and bishops toward the center](openings/develop-pieces-toward-the-center.md) (beginner): Bring your minor pieces out to squares facing the center.
- [Develop your knights before your bishops](openings/develop-knights-before-bishops.md) (beginner): Bring the knights out first.
- [Put your knights on the central squares of your third rank](openings/develop-knights-to-central-third-rank-squares.md) (beginner): Develop knights to the central squares of your third rank (sixth rank as Black), such as f6, not to the edge or to e7.
- [Castle early, ideally within the first 5 to 10 moves](openings/castle-early-to-get-your-king-safe.md) (beginner): Get your king out of the center by castling early.
- [Don't trade your bishop for a knight without a good reason](openings/do-not-trade-a-bishop-for-a-knight-without-a-reason.md) (beginner): When your bishop is challenged, don't swap it for their knight by reflex.
- [Don't develop your queen too early](openings/do-not-bring-your-queen-out-early.md) (beginner): Keep the queen at home until your knights and bishops are out.
- [Don't move the same piece twice in the opening](openings/do-not-move-the-same-piece-twice-in-the-opening.md) (beginner): In the opening, bring out a new piece rather than moving one that's already developed.
- [Don't open the center while you're behind in development](openings/do-not-open-the-center-while-behind-in-development.md) (beginner): If most of your pieces are still at home and your king hasn't castled, avoid captures that open the central files.
- [Don't spend opening moves on flank pawns without a reason](openings/do-not-waste-opening-moves-on-flank-pawns.md) (beginner): In the opening, move your central pawns (e, d, and often c) and develop pieces.
- [Think twice before moving your f-pawn](openings/think-twice-before-moving-the-f-pawn.md) (beginner): Pushing the f-pawn can support the center, but it opens a diagonal to your king.
- [After developing and castling, put your queen on a central second-rank square](openings/develop-the-queen-to-the-second-rank.md) (beginner): Once your center pawns, minor pieces and castling are done, bring the queen to a central square on your second rank, where it's safe but still in the game.
- [Connect your rooks by clearing your back rank](openings/connect-your-rooks.md) (beginner): Finish development by clearing every piece from between your rooks.
- [Answer a hasty attack with a counterstrike in the center](openings/answer-a-hasty-attack-with-a-central-counterstrike.md) (beginner): When an opponent plays something unusual and attacks early, don't just retreat and let them keep going.
- [Control the center with pieces, not only with pawns](openings/control-the-center-with-pieces-not-only-pawns.md) (intermediate): The classic advice is to put pawns in the center, but pieces can control it from a distance.
- [If you let your opponent build a big pawn center, attack it immediately](openings/let-them-take-the-center-only-if-you-attack-it-at-once.md) (intermediate): Giving up the center is only OK if you start hitting it right away with pawns and pieces.

## Tactics

- [Guard f7 (f2 as White): it's a vulnerable point early in the game](tactics/watch-the-weak-f7-and-f2-squares.md) (beginner): Early in the game, the pawn on f7 (f2 for White) is a common target.
- [Watch for a queen on b3 hitting f7 and b7 at once](tactics/watch-for-the-queen-double-attack-on-f7-and-b7.md) (beginner): A queen landing on b3 can attack the f7 and b7 pawns together, and Black often can't cover both.
- [Count attackers and defenders before you move a piece or pawn to a square](tactics/count-attackers-and-defenders-before-you-commit.md) (beginner): Before putting a piece or pawn on a square, count how many enemy pieces attack it and how many of yours defend it.
- [Keep your pieces defended even before they're attacked](tactics/keep-your-pieces-defended-before-they-are-attacked.md) (beginner): Don't wait for a threat before protecting a piece.
- [Don't count a pinned piece as a defender](tactics/a-piece-pinned-to-the-king-cannot-capture-or-guard.md) (beginner): A piece pinned to its king can't leave the line of the pin, so it can't capture or protect anything off that line (a pinned knight, as in both source examples, can't move at all).
- [Guard your back rank against mate](tactics/guard-your-back-rank-against-mate.md) (beginner): Once your rooks and queen leave your first rank, a king sheltered behind its pawns can be mated by an enemy rook or queen landing on that rank.
- [Check whether a defender is doing two jobs at once](tactics/an-overworked-defender-can-be-overloaded.md) (intermediate): A defended piece isn't automatically safe.

## Strategy

- [Count material with the 1-3-3-5-9 piece values before trading](strategy/count-material-with-standard-piece-values.md) (beginner): Pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9.
- [When you have no clear plan, make a move that gains space in the center](strategy/with-no-plan-gain-space-in-the-center.md) (beginner): If your pieces are developed and you see no tactic and no long-term plan, look for a move that increases your influence, especially in the center.
- [When you have the initiative, keep making threats: no slow moves](strategy/keep-making-threats-while-you-have-the-initiative.md) (beginner): If you're the one making threats your opponent must answer, keep it going with forcing, active moves.
- [Bring every piece into the game before pushing ahead](strategy/bring-every-piece-into-play.md) (beginner): Don't leave pieces 'on the bench'.
- [Trade pieces when you're ahead in material](strategy/trade-pieces-when-ahead-in-material.md) (beginner): When you're up material, swap pieces off to simplify.
- [Against a stronger player, don't swap pieces just to 'simplify'](strategy/do-not-trade-just-to-simplify-against-stronger-players.md) (beginner): Swapping pieces just to simplify against a stronger opponent often helps them, because stronger players are often relatively stronger in the endgame, while mistakes are more common in a complicated middlegame.
- [Put your rooks on open and half-open files](strategy/put-rooks-on-open-and-half-open-files.md) (beginner): Rooks need open lines.
- [Prefer bishops in open positions and knights in closed ones](strategy/bishops-prefer-open-positions-knights-closed.md) (intermediate): Bishops and knights are worth about the same, but bishops are stronger when lines are open and knights are stronger when pawns block the board.
- [Plant a knight on an outpost where enemy pawns can't attack it](strategy/put-knights-on-outposts-pawns-cannot-attack.md) (intermediate): Knights are easily chased away by pawns, so look for a square in the enemy position that no enemy pawn can attack, ideally supported by one of your pawns, and put a knight there.
- [Keep your opponent's pieces as passive as you can](strategy/keep-your-opponents-pieces-inactive.md) (intermediate): Part of every plan is restricting the opponent.
- [Learn the typical middlegame plans that go with each opening you play](strategy/study-each-opening-with-its-middlegame-plans.md) (intermediate): Once the opening is over, you have to make plans from the features of the position, and the pawn structure largely shapes them.
- [Avoid creating pawn weaknesses unless you get something for them](strategy/avoid-pawn-weaknesses-without-compensation.md) (intermediate): Pawn moves can't be taken back, so weaknesses like isolated, doubled or backward pawns and holes usually last all game.

## Endgames

- [Checkmate with two rooks (or queens) using the staircase](endgames/mate-with-two-heavy-pieces-using-the-staircase.md) (beginner): Use one heavy piece to cut the king off along a rank or file, check with the other to push it back one line, and repeat until it's mated at the edge.
- [When you're winning, check every move for stalemate](endgames/check-for-stalemate-when-far-ahead.md) (beginner): Before any move that doesn't give check, make sure your opponent will still have a move.
- [Push your passed pawns](endgames/push-your-passed-pawns.md) (beginner): A passed pawn has no enemy pawn on its own file or the files next to it to stop it.
- [Push two connected passed pawns together](endgames/push-connected-passed-pawns-together.md) (beginner): Two passed pawns side by side are, in the endgame video's words, an 'unstoppable force'.
- [In the endgame, use your king as an active piece](endgames/activate-your-king-in-the-endgame.md) (beginner): Once most pieces are off the board, bring your king toward the center and use it to attack.
- [Know which pieces can checkmate a lone king](endgames/know-which-material-can-mate-a-lone-king.md) (beginner): A king plus a rook or queen can mate a lone king.
- [No passed pawn? Make creating one your plan](endgames/with-no-passed-pawn-plan-to-create-one.md) (intermediate): In a quiet endgame with no obvious plan, pick the pawn most likely to become passed and work step by step to make it passed.
- [Cut off the enemy king with your rook](endgames/cut-off-the-enemy-king-with-a-rook.md) (intermediate): Use your rook to keep the enemy king on its back rank, away from the action, then bring your own king in.
- [Take the opposition to make the enemy king give way](endgames/take-the-opposition-in-king-and-pawn-endings.md) (intermediate): In king and pawn endings, place your king facing the enemy king with one square between them.
- [Expect a draw when the only bishops left run on opposite colors](endgames/opposite-colored-bishop-endings-are-drawish.md) (intermediate): In an ending where each side has only a bishop (plus pawns) and the bishops run on different colors, the defender can often build a fortress and draw even two pawns down.
- [Put rooks behind passed pawns, yours or the opponent's](endgames/put-rooks-behind-passed-pawns.md) (intermediate): A rook belongs behind a passed pawn.
- [Beat a pawn about to promote with zigzag queen checks](endgames/stop-an-advanced-pawn-with-zigzag-queen-checks.md) (intermediate): With a queen against a pawn one step from promoting, give zigzag checks until the enemy king has to stand in front of its own pawn.

## Thinking process

- [Before each move, ask what your opponent wants to do](thinking-process/ask-what-your-opponent-wants-before-you-move.md) (beginner): Every move, ask 'what is my opponent trying to do?' Spot their threats and plans early, and stop them before you pursue your own.
- [When you see a good move, look for a better one](thinking-process/when-you-see-a-good-move-look-for-a-better-one.md) (beginner): Don't play the first good move you find.
- [Make tactics your first study priority](thinking-process/prioritize-tactics-below-master-level.md) (beginner): Below master level, games are decided by tactics (move-by-move threats and defenses) more often than by strategy, which is why many coaches treat studying tactics as the most efficient way to improve your results.
