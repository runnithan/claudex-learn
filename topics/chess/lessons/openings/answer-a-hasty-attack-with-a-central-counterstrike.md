# Answer a hasty attack with a counterstrike in the center

## TL;DR

When an opponent plays something unusual and attacks early, don't just retreat and let them keep going. Hit back in the center, often with a pawn push, and take the initiative.

## Why it matters

In the opening video, an opponent attacks early with a pawn on e5 that chases your knight. If you just move the knight, they grab a pawn, attack the knight again, and you may already be lost. Instead, ...d5 counterattacks their bishop, stops their expansion and takes the initiative. In a second example, against an early f4 attack, ...d5 again gives the opponent the problems: if White takes and you recapture with the knight, a pawn is left hanging; if White then takes on e5 it loses to a double attack, and if White takes on d5 instead you recapture and attack the pawns on g2 and f4.

## How to apply

When you face an unfamiliar attack, look first for a central pawn break (the video uses ...d5) that hits back at their pieces or their center. The video's point is that opening principles are universal rules: even without knowing the theory, a counterstrike in the center is a reliable answer.

## Check yourself

Early in a game you don't recognize, your opponent pushes a pawn to attack your knight and seems ready to keep pushing. Before simply retreating the knight, what does this lesson tell you to look for?
