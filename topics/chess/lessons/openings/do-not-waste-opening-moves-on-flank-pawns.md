# Don't spend opening moves on flank pawns without a reason

## TL;DR

In the opening, move your central pawns (e, d, and often c) and develop pieces. Moves like a3, h3 or ...h6 usually waste time.

## Why it matters

The opening video says moving the pawns on the far left and right of the board is often pointless. Moving the g-pawn to develop a bishop is slower than developing the bishop along its diagonal in one move. The video calls moves like h3 and a3 often unnecessary. Playing h3 to stop a knight or bishop landing on g4 is usually pointless, because that piece isn't really dangerous there and you can kick it away later. In the thinking-process video, Black's ...h6 (played to stop a knight check) is called slightly inaccurate: developing a bishop would have been better.

## How to apply

Before a flank pawn move in the opening, ask what it does that a developing move wouldn't. Don't defend in advance against a piece that might land on g4: deal with it if it comes. The central pawn moves (e and d, and often c to reinforce the center) are the ones worth spending a tempo on. The f-pawn has its own lesson.

## Check yourself

It's move 5 and you're thinking of playing h3 'so their bishop can never come to g4'. Two of your minor pieces are undeveloped. What does this lesson say?

## Caveat

The video's example of wasted time is moving the g-pawn to develop a bishop. The Wikipedia article treats developing a bishop from the side of the board (a fianchetto) as a standard hypermodern way to control the center from afar, and says a fianchettoed bishop can keep the king well defended. So a flank pawn move made to fianchetto isn't automatically a waste. The warning fits flank pawn moves with no clear purpose.
