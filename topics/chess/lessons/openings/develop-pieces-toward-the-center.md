# Develop your knights and bishops toward the center

## TL;DR

Bring your minor pieces out to squares facing the center. A centralized piece controls far more squares than one on the edge.

## Why it matters

The opening video compares pieces directly. A centralized bishop sweeps two long diagonals while a badly placed one does almost nothing. A knight on f3 controls eight squares while a knight on h6 controls only four, so the centralized knight is twice as strong. Wikipedia makes the same point about the edge: a knight at the edge or corner controls fewer squares than one in the interior ('a knight on the rim is dim').

## How to apply

When choosing a developing move, count the squares the piece will control from its new square and prefer the one nearer the center, like the video's f3 knight (eight squares) over an h6 knight (four). Development and center control go together: the article lists both among the fundamental aims of most openings.

## Check yourself

You can develop your knight to a square on the edge of the board or to a square closer to the center. Using this lesson, how would you compare the two, and which do you pick?
