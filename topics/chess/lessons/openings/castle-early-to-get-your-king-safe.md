# Castle early, ideally within the first 5 to 10 moves

## TL;DR

Get your king out of the center by castling early. A king left in the middle is in danger as soon as the center opens.

## Why it matters

In the opening video's example, Black delayed castling to develop a knight to d7, which looked reasonable. White broke open the center with e5, then played Bxf7+ to draw the king out and Ng5+, a check that was also a discovered attack. In the main lines Black loses the queen, and in the game Black was finished off within a few moves. Wikipedia notes that in the middlegame the king is often best protected in a corner behind its pawns, which is what castling achieves.

## How to apply

Aim to castle within the first 5 to 10 moves (the video's target). The thinking-process video follows the same opening routine: control the center, develop the minor pieces, castle the king. Wikipedia adds that castling also puts a rook on a central file, strengthens the back rank and often helps connect the rooks. Until you've castled, be wary of moves that let the center open.
