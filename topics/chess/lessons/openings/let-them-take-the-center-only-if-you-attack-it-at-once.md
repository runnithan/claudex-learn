# If you let your opponent build a big pawn center, attack it immediately

## TL;DR

Giving up the center is only OK if you start hitting it right away with pawns and pieces. If you neglect it, the center wins.

## Why it matters

The opening video's example is the Grünfeld. White gets a big, solid-looking pawn center, but Black is fine because it attacks at once with ...c5 and ...Bg7, then adds the knight, and can follow with a pin (...Bg4) and ...Qa5 against a pawn. If Black instead played slow moves like ...b6 and ...Bb7, that would be neglecting the center and White would have a strong advantage. Wikipedia describes the broader idea: in some openings one player accepts less space for a time to set up a counterattack in the middlegame, one of the concepts behind hypermodern play. The video's point is that against a big pawn center the counterattack has to start at once.

## How to apply

If you allow a pawn center, plan your next few moves around attacking it: strike with a pawn, add pieces against the same target, pin its defenders, and bring pressure with the queen. If you can't do that at once, occupy the center yourself instead.

## Check yourself

Your opponent has pawns on d4 and e4 and you have none in the center. You're considering a slow plan of developing on the queenside. What does this lesson say about that choice?
