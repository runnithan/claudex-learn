# Don't move the same piece twice in the opening

## TL;DR

In the opening, bring out a new piece rather than moving one that's already developed. Shuffling a developed piece leaves the rest of your army behind.

## Why it matters

In the opening video, Black moved a knight a second time (to d4), hoping for a trap. White ignored it and Black had to trade. White then had three useful moves made while Black was still undeveloped. When White attacked the center with e5, with Qxf7 mate in the air, White was already winning. The video's point is that moving an already-developed knight changes little, while bringing in another piece adds real firepower.

## How to apply

On each opening move, ask whether a piece still on its home square could come out instead. Don't spend moves setting traps with a piece that's already developed. In the video's queen example, Black's pieces came out while attacking something (the queen, then a bishop with ...Ne5), so each move gained time.

## Check yourself

It's move 6. Your knight is already on f6, and you see a knight jump that sets a small trap but attacks nothing. Your bishop and other knight are still at home. What does this lesson suggest, and when would moving the knight again be justified?

## Caveat

Treat this as a default, not an absolute. In the thinking-process video's opening, both sides move pieces again for a reason. White's bishop went to c4 to attack the knight on d5, and in the line the video calls best, ...Nb6 hits the bishop and it retreats to b3. Black's knight went to f6, captured on d5, and in the game captured again on c3 (the video names ...Nb6 as the best third knight move). Captures, retreats from an attack and moves that gain a tempo can all justify moving a piece twice.
