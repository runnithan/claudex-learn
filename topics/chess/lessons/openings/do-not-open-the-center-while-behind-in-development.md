# Don't open the center while you're behind in development

## TL;DR

If most of your pieces are still at home and your king hasn't castled, avoid captures that open the central files. Open lines favor the side with more pieces out.

## Why it matters

In the opening video's example, Black is mostly undeveloped and considers capturing White's e4 pawn, which opens the central files. White answers with Re1 hitting the knight, wins back the e5 pawn and brings a rook in with check. After the natural-looking ...Nxb5, White plays Nd5 and exploits Black's king, which is stuck in the open center. A pinned bishop can't escape, Black's attempt to castle out of trouble fails, and Qh5 sets up a mating attack.

## How to apply

Before a capture or pawn move that opens a central file, compare development: how many of your pieces are out, and is your king castled? If you're behind, develop and castle first. The video admits that in its own example the capture (...Nxe4) is a decent, engine-approved move, but one that needs precise defense afterwards. The principle is about the risk, not a rule that the capture always loses.

## Check yourself

You've developed one knight and haven't castled. Your opponent has three pieces out and has castled. You can win a central pawn with a capture that opens the e-file toward your king. What does this lesson say to weigh before taking?
