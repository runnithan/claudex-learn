# After developing and castling, put your queen on a central second-rank square

## TL;DR

Once your center pawns, minor pieces and castling are done, bring the queen to a central square on your second rank, where it's safe but still in the game.

## Why it matters

The opening video shows a queen on the second rank supporting a central pawn advance or a plan to trade off an enemy bishop, while no enemy piece can attack it. Put it further forward and it can be hit: in the video's line, the opponent trades and then pushes a6 and b5, and the advanced queen is in danger.

## How to apply

The order is: center pawns, minor pieces, castle, then the queen to a central square on your second rank (seventh for Black). Pick the square that supports your next central pawn move. This also clears the back rank so your rooks connect.

## Check yourself

You've castled and developed all four minor pieces. You can put your queen on an advanced square in the middle of the board, or one step up on a central second-rank square. Which does this lesson recommend, and what risk does the other carry?
