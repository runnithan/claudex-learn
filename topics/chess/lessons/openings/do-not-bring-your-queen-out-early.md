# Don't develop your queen too early

## TL;DR

Keep the queen at home until your knights and bishops are out. An early queen gets chased, and every chase lets the opponent develop a piece with tempo.

## Why it matters

In the opening video's example, White brought the queen out early after a central pawn exchange. Black then developed minor pieces while attacking it. The queen retreated, a black knight jumped out to hit a bishop, and then ...Bb4+ followed by a bishop move back attacked the queen again. White couldn't take the bishop because of a royal fork, and Qg3 lost to ...Bxf2+, with a fork waiting after either recapture. Wikipedia gives the reason: the queen is powerful but easily harassed, so it is generally wise to wait until the knights and bishops are developed, so that minor pieces can't attack it and gain time.

## How to apply

Develop knights and bishops first. Before an early queen move, ask whether an enemy minor piece could attack it next move while developing. The lesson on queen placement gives the usual target once you're developed: a central square on your second rank.

## Check yourself

On move 4 you could recapture a central pawn with your queen, putting it in the middle of the board where your opponent's undeveloped knight and bishop could hit it. What does this lesson warn will happen?
