# Occupy the center with pawns in the opening

## TL;DR

Early in the game, put pawns on the central squares when your opponent lets you. Central pawns control the middle of the board, cramp the opponent's pieces and make your own development easy.

## Why it matters

In the opening video, White gets pawns on two of the four central squares while Black just develops pieces. White's pawns cover so many squares in the middle that Black's pieces can't come forward, and whenever a black knight lands in front of them a pawn push kicks it back. Meanwhile White's bishops and queen move around freely. The Wikipedia article adds that tactical battles often happen around the central squares, where pieces can reach most of the board, so center control gives you more room for both attack and defense.

## How to apply

After 1.e4, if your opponent doesn't contest the center, add a second central pawn (the video's example). Ask two questions: which central squares do my pawns control, and can a pawn push chase an enemy knight away? Openings aim to control the center while developing pieces. There is an exception, covered in the hypermodern lessons: you can also control the center with pieces from a distance.

## Check yourself

You're White after 1.e4 and your opponent replies with a quiet flank move that leaves the center alone. Following this lesson, what kind of move should you look for next, and what will it do to their knights?
