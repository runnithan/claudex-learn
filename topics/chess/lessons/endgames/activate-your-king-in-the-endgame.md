# In the endgame, use your king as an active piece

## TL;DR

Once most pieces are off the board, bring your king toward the center and use it to attack. Treat it like a knight you'd never leave on its home square.

## Why it matters

The endgame video swaps a king and a knight on the board to make the point: you'd never leave a knight idle on g8, so don't leave your king there either. In its example, Black's king marches to the center, wins one pawn, then another, and the passed pawns decide the game. Wikipedia: with less material on the board a quick checkmate is less of a worry, and a centralized king protects its own pawns, attacks the opponent's and gets in the way of the enemy king. The thinking-process video shows the other side of this: White avoided a capture that would have let Black's king become active and grab pawns.

## How to apply

When the queens and most other pieces are gone, first bring your king to the center, then look for something to attack with it, such as loose pawns. Deny the enemy king the same activity.

## Check yourself

Queens and rooks are off the board. Your king is still on g1 behind its pawns, while the enemy king heads for the center. What does this lesson say to do?

## Caveat

The sources give slightly different numbers for the endgame king. The video says it's as strong as a knight (3 points on Wikipedia's scale), while Wikipedia puts its fighting value at about 4 points in one place and 3.5 in another. The practical advice is the same.
