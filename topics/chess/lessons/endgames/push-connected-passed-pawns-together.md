# Push two connected passed pawns together

## TL;DR

Two passed pawns side by side are, in the endgame video's words, an 'unstoppable force'. Push them together, and bring your king up first when a push would drop one.

## Why it matters

The endgame video gives three reasons. The pawns cover the squares in front of each other, so they can't be blockaded. If one is attacked, the other can advance to protect it. And if the opponent gives up a rook for one pawn, the other still becomes a queen. The thinking-process video won its game with two connected passed pawns, stressing that they should be pushed as fast as possible because each step makes them more dangerous and restricts the opponent more.

## How to apply

Advance the pawns side by side. If a push would leave one pawn hanging, move your king forward first (the endgame video's advice), then push. Keep going: the further they get, the more they tie down the opponent's pieces.

## Check yourself

You have connected passed pawns on the c- and d-files. Pushing the c-pawn now would leave the d-pawn attacked and unprotected. What does this lesson say to do?

## Caveat

The endgame video states flatly that two connected passed pawns win the game. Wikipedia only calls passed pawns strong, especially when protected by another pawn. In the thinking-process video's winning ending, White still had to deal with a back-rank mate threat while the pawns advanced. Treat it as a very strong rule of thumb, not a guarantee.
