# Push your passed pawns

## TL;DR

A passed pawn has no enemy pawn on its own file or the files next to it to stop it. Push it: the further it goes, the more it ties down the opponent's pieces.

## Why it matters

The endgame video calls this the main endgame plan (its '3P rule': push passed pawns). Pushing a passed pawn forces the opponent's pieces into passive defense, and if it can't promote right away, you bring more pieces to support it until it does. The thinking-process video adds that the further a passed pawn advances, the more it restricts the opponent and the bigger your advantage. Wikipedia: passed pawns are strong in the endgame, especially when advanced or protected by another pawn, and a passed pawn on the sixth rank is roughly as strong as a knight or bishop.

## How to apply

Once you have a passed pawn, make pushing it your plan. Before each push, check that the square in front is safe (see the lesson on counting attackers and defenders). If it isn't, bring a piece to support the push first, then push.

## Check yourself

In an endgame you have a passed pawn on the fourth rank and your opponent's rook is passive. What plan does this lesson give you, and what do you do if the next square is covered by an enemy piece?
