# Know which pieces can checkmate a lone king

## TL;DR

A king plus a rook or queen can mate a lone king. A king plus one bishop or one knight cannot. Two bishops mate easily, bishop and knight can but it's hard, and two knights can't force it.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia lists these basic facts. They tell you whether a simplified endgame can still be won: a lone knight or bishop is not enough material.

## How to apply

King and queen: mates. King and rook: mates, a little harder than with the queen. King and two bishops: mates easily. King, bishop and knight: can force mate, the hardest of the basic mates. King and two knights: can mate but can't force it. King and one knight, or king and one bishop: can't mate. Keep this in mind when trading down against a bare king.

## Check yourself

In an endgame with no pawns left, you can trade your last rook for their last rook, leaving you with king and one knight against a lone king. What does this lesson say about the result?
