# Cut off the enemy king with your rook

## TL;DR

Use your rook to keep the enemy king on its back rank, away from the action, then bring your own king in. You'll be fighting with two active pieces against one.

## Why it matters

The endgame video calls this the flip side of activating your king. In its example, Rd7 attacks a pawn, but more importantly it cuts the black king off on the last rank so it can't come to the center. White's king then walks forward, and White is likely to win because it has two pieces in the game against one. Wikipedia: rooks on the seventh rank attack pawns and can confine the enemy king to its back rank, and two rooks there are often a sign of a winning position. In the thinking-process video, White's Rb7+ activated the rook and forced Black's king back.

## How to apply

In rook endgames, look for a rank or file where your rook stops the enemy king from coming toward the center or your pawns (often your seventh rank). Keep the rook there and walk your own king forward.
