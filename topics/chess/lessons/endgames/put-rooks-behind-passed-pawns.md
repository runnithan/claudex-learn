# Put rooks behind passed pawns, yours or the opponent's

## TL;DR

A rook belongs behind a passed pawn. Behind your own, it guards the pawn and supports its advance; behind the enemy's, it attacks the pawn and covers every square in front of it.

## Why it matters

In the endgame video, Black's passed pawn is attacked by Rd5. Moving Black's rook behind the pawn guards it and supports its advance all the way, and Black wins. The natural-looking alternative, pushing the pawn out of danger, is a big mistake: White's rook gets behind it, attacks it and indirectly controls all the squares in front, so the pawn can never advance and the game is drawn. Wikipedia calls this Tarrasch's rule: rooks, whether friend or foe of the pawn, are usually strongest behind it.

## How to apply

Whenever there's a passed pawn on the board, ask where the rooks should be. For your own passed pawn, put your rook behind it. For the opponent's, get your rook behind it. Don't push a pawn out of an attack if that lets the enemy rook get behind it.

## Check yourself

Your passed pawn is attacked from the side by an enemy rook. You can push the pawn forward to escape, or move your rook behind it. Which does this lesson recommend, and what goes wrong with the other?
