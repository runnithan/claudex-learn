# Take the opposition to make the enemy king give way

## TL;DR

In king and pawn endings, place your king facing the enemy king with one square between them. Their king then has to step aside, and you go around the other way.

## Why it matters

The endgame video: when the kings face each other one square apart, the opponent is in zugzwang, meaning any move makes their position worse. They have to give you space, so you can go around them and win their pawns. That's how a symmetrical, drawish-looking position can be an easy win. In the video's test position, Black's natural pawn push (...e3) throws the win away and only draws. Taking the opposition first forces the white king aside, then Black's king goes to the other side and nothing can stop the pawn. Wikipedia notes that zugzwang is often a factor in endgames. In its example, Black to move has to let White queen, while White to move can only draw.

## How to apply

Before pushing your pawn in a king and pawn ending, see whether you can put your king directly opposite theirs with one square between them. When their king steps to one side, step to the other side and go forward. Don't rush the pawn push while the enemy king still blocks your way.

## Check yourself

King and pawn endgame, your move. You can push your passed pawn right away, or move your king to face the enemy king with one square in between. According to this lesson, which comes first, and what do you do after their king steps aside?
