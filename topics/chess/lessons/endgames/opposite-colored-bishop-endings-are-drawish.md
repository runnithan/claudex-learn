# Expect a draw when the only bishops left run on opposite colors

## TL;DR

In an ending where each side has only a bishop (plus pawns) and the bishops run on different colors, the defender can often build a fortress and draw even two pawns down. Aim for it when you're behind; avoid it when you're ahead.

## Why it matters

In the endgame video's example, White is two pawns up with a dangerous-looking passed pawn, yet Black draws, even giving up a third pawn. Black controls all the squares of its own bishop's color, White's dark-squared bishop can never attack light squares, and Black simply moves the bishop back and forth. Wikipedia agrees: these endgames are frequently drawn even with one or two extra pawns, so trading down into one usually helps only the weaker side.

## How to apply

When you're ahead in material, don't trade into an ending where your bishop and theirs run on different colors. When you're behind, look for trades that reach one, then set up so you control the squares of your bishop's color and just move the bishop back and forth.

## Check yourself

You're two pawns up and can trade into an ending where your bishop is light-squared and theirs is dark-squared, or you can keep more pieces on. What does this lesson say about the trade?
