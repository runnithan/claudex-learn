# Guard f7 (f2 as White): it's a vulnerable point early in the game

## TL;DR

Early in the game, the pawn on f7 (f2 for White) is a common target. Before each opening move, check whether the opponent can hit it.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia calls f7 and f2 vulnerable squares in the opening, which is why the bishop on the same color as the enemy king is a little more valuable there. The videos keep showing it. In the opening video's castling example, White wins with Bxf7+, drawing the king out, then Ng5+ with a discovered attack. In the thinking-process video, a natural-looking ...Bg4 would have lost to Bxf7+ followed by Ng5+, winning the bishop. Later in that game the author calls f7 the pawn Black most had to defend, because otherwise the king would be dragged into the center.

## How to apply

In the opening, ask which enemy pieces aim at f7 (or f2). In these sources the attackers are a bishop on c4 or b3, a queen coming to b3 or landing on f7 itself, and a knight jumping to g5. Before a natural developing move, check whether it leaves f7 short of defenders or allows a sacrifice there that pulls your king out. Castling early reduces the danger.

## Check yourself

You're Black. White has a bishop on c4 and a knight on f3, and you're about to pin that knight with ...Bg4. Based on this lesson, what should you check before playing it?
