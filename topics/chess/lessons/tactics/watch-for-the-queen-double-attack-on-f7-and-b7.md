# Watch for a queen on b3 hitting f7 and b7 at once

## TL;DR

A queen landing on b3 can attack the f7 and b7 pawns together, and Black often can't cover both. Look for it as White, and check for it as Black.

## Why it matters

In the thinking-process video, Black's natural developing move ...Bf5 was a losing blunder because of Qb3, a double attack on f7 and b7. Black blocked the attack on f7 with ...e6, White took on b7 threatening to win a knight, and Black was already in deep trouble. In the opening video, after Black traded its light-squared bishop for a knight, the same Qb3 hit f7 and b7 and Black was already lost: defending one pawn dropped the other, with a rook hanging on a8 or a pin on the queen coming.

## How to apply

Whenever a queen can reach b3, look at f7 and b7 together: are both defended, and can one move cover both? In both source games, Black's light-squared bishop had already left its home square (traded off in one, developed to f5 in the other) when Qb3 came. As Black, check for this before moving that bishop. As White, look for the chance.

## Check yourself

You're Black and about to develop your light-squared bishop to f5. White's queen can reach b3 in one move and White's bishop points at f7. What should you check first, according to this lesson?
