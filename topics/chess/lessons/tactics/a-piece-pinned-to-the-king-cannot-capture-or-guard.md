# Don't count a pinned piece as a defender

## TL;DR

A piece pinned to its king can't leave the line of the pin, so it can't capture or protect anything off that line (a pinned knight, as in both source examples, can't move at all). Leave it out when you count defenders, and look for pins that switch off your opponent's defenders.

## Why it matters

In the thinking-process video, White adds another attacker with Ne5, and Black can't take it because the black piece that could capture is pinned: 'He cannot take because he would lose his king.' In the opening video, ...Bb4 pins White's knight so that it no longer controls the central squares, and White can't push e4 because Black would simply win the pawn.

## How to apply

When you count attackers and defenders, check each defender: is it pinned to its king? If so, it can't recapture off the line of the pin, so leave it out (a pinned knight can't recapture at all). When you're attacking, look for a pin (like the video's ...Bb4 against a knight) that stops a piece from guarding the square or pawn you're after.

## Check yourself

You're counting the defenders of one of your pawns: a knight and a bishop. But the knight is pinned to your king by an enemy bishop. How many real defenders does the pawn have, according to this lesson?

## Caveat

From outside these sources: a pinned piece still guards squares against the enemy king. If the only thing defending your piece is pinned, the enemy king still can't capture it, because the king can't move into check.
