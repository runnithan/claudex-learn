# Count attackers and defenders before you move a piece or pawn to a square

## TL;DR

Before putting a piece or pawn on a square, count how many enemy pieces attack it and how many of yours defend it. If they have more, add a defender first.

## Why it matters

In the endgame video, White wants to push a pawn to c5, but Black attacks that square twice (with a pawn and a rook). Pushing straight away just loses the pawn, so White first brings the rook over to add another attacker on c5, and only then pushes. Wikipedia states the rule: when more pieces attack, the number of defenders must go up too, and the pieces' values have to be taken into account. The thinking-process video uses the same counting to attack: with two attackers already on a black knight, the author asks 'how do you get more attackers into the game?' and brings in another piece.

## How to apply

For any square you want to use, or any of your pieces that's attacked: list their attackers, list your defenders, and compare. Weigh the values of the pieces as well, not just the count. If the count is against you, add a defender (or an attacker, when you're the one attacking) before you commit.
