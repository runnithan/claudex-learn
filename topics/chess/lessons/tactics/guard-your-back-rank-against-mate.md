# Guard your back rank against mate

## TL;DR

Once your rooks and queen leave your first rank, a king sheltered behind its pawns can be mated by an enemy rook or queen landing on that rank. Watch for it, and give your king an escape square in time.

## Why it matters

Wikipedia describes the back-rank mate and its fix. Moving one of the pawns in front of the king (making 'luft') gives it an escape square, but can weaken its safety in other ways, so it's a trade-off. In the thinking-process video's endgame, Black's only real threat was a back-rank mate. White played g3 early as a precaution against it, later used a bishop to cover e1, and didn't grab a pawn with the rook when that would have allowed mate in one.

## How to apply

When your rooks and queen leave the back rank, ask: can an enemy rook or queen reach my first rank with check, and would my king have a square to go to? If not, cover the entry square with a piece, or make luft by moving one pawn in front of the king. Choose the moment with care, because it can weaken the king. Before capturing with a rook or queen, check that it doesn't leave your back rank undefended.

## Check yourself

Your king is castled with three pawns in front of it, your rook has just left the back rank, and your opponent's rook can reach your first rank. Before your next move, what two options does this lesson give you?
