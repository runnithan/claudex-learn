# When you see a good move, look for a better one

## TL;DR

Don't play the first good move you find. Look again for a move that does more, like a move that does several jobs and also creates a threat.

## Why it matters

The thinking-process video quotes this rule at the moment White could simply capture a rook. Taking at once would have helped Black's king become active in the endgame. Instead White found d4, a multi-purpose move: it opened a line for the dark-squared bishop, controlled central squares, and threatened d5, winning the rook without giving back the bishop.

## How to apply

When you find a good move (a capture, a threat), pause and ask whether another move achieves the same and more. Look for moves that do several jobs at once. Compare what each option leaves your opponent: for example, does the capture activate their king?

## Check yourself

You spot a capture that wins the exchange. What does this lesson tell you to do before playing it, and what would make another move 'better'?
