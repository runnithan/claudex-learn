# Before each move, ask what your opponent wants to do

## TL;DR

Every move, ask 'what is my opponent trying to do?' Spot their threats and plans early, and stop them before you pursue your own.

## Why it matters

The thinking-process video (the author's game with zero blunders) keeps coming back to this question: 'Always need to think what is the opponent looking to do?' White saw that Black wanted to bring out a bishop and put a rook on the only open file, then that Black wanted to activate the king, and played moves to stop each plan. White spotted Black's only long-term threat, a back-rank mate, and played g3 in time. Later, when a black bishop move attacked a pawn and threatened mate in one, White didn't grab a pawn with the rook, which would have allowed back-rank mate.

## How to apply

After your opponent moves, and before choosing your own move: 1. What does their last move threaten (captures, checks, mate)? 2. What are they trying to achieve over the next few moves (a piece to an open file, activating the king, an attack)? 3. Pick a move that meets the threat, and if possible also stops the plan. Only then pursue your own idea.

## Check yourself

Your opponent has just moved a bishop. You're about to capture a free-looking pawn with your rook. What questions does this lesson say to ask first?
